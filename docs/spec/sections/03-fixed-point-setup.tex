\section{The fixed-point setup}
\label{sec:setup}

This section makes the circularity precise. It names the two functions
that close the loop (the sync map $F$ and the conservative BPT
valuation $P_A$), states the fixed-point equation the kernel's sync must
solve on every pool operation, and defines the error function the
on-chain solver targets.

\subsection{The sync map $F$}
\label{sec:setup:F}

Each sync, the Dusk kernel calls the shared Royco accountant function
\code{previewSyncTrancheAccounting}, which we denote $F$. Given the
current raw NAVs of both tranches, $F$ runs the attribution and
settlement steps of \cref{sec:overview:waterfall} against the stored
checkpoint and returns the effective NAVs:
\[
  F\colon \bigl(\STraw,\ \JTraw;\ \text{checkpoint}\bigr) \longmapsto (\STeff,\ \JTeff).
\]
Two structural properties of $F$ are stated in \cref{sec:overview:waterfall}
and proved in \cref{sec:existence}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{NAV conservation:} $\STraw + \JTraw \equiv \STeff + \JTeff$.
  \item \textbf{Piecewise-affine:} each branch is affine in its inputs with
    slope in $[0, 1]$, and the branches are joined continuously at
    their kinks.
\end{enumerate}

In a Dusk market the senior raw NAV is exogenous to the loop: it is the
value of the senior tranche's own underlying assets, marked to market
independently of the pool. Only the junior raw NAV depends on the senior
rate, because the junior's collateral is the BPT.
The senior side therefore drops out of the loop, and we may regard $F$ as a
function of one variable, $\JTraw$, holding $\STraw$ and the checkpoint
fixed for the duration of a single sync:
\[
  F\colon \JTraw \longmapsto \STeff.
\]
The kernel reads $\STraw$ once per sync and feeds it into every invocation
of $F$ during the bisection.

\subsection{The conservative BPT valuation $P_A$}
\label{sec:setup:PA}

The junior tranche's collateral is the BPT of the Gyro E-CLP pool, of
which it owns a fraction $f = \mathrm{jtBPT} / \mathrm{totalBPT}$.
The pool's two legs are the senior tranche share and a quote asset, with
raw reserves $b_{s,\text{raw}}$ and $b_{q,\text{raw}}$.

Under the chosen architecture (\cref{sec:overview:pieces}), the senior
share is registered as a Balancer V3 \code{WITH_RATE} token, so Balancer
scales the senior leg's live balance by the rate $y$ at pool-load time
before any pool math runs:
\begin{align*}
  b_s(y) &= b_{s,\text{raw}} \cdot y, \\
  b_q    &= b_{q,\text{raw}} \cdot q,
\end{align*}
where $q$ is the quote-asset rate ($q = 1$ for a non-rate-bearing quote).
The E-CLP invariant $L$ is then computed from the rate-scaled balances.

Given the invariant, the pool's \emph{conservative} TVL is its value at
the fair reserves implied by the invariant and the rate: the point on
the curve where the marginal price equals $y$. Because this depends only
on $L$ and $y$, not on the live composition, value-free swap
manipulation cannot move it (a swap conserves $L$ in the fee-free pool
math, and the LP fee it leaves behind only grows $L$). The external E-CLP price ratio is
$[1, 1]$: the rate is injected through the senior leg's live balance, and
double-injecting it as an external price would over-count.

Write the conservative TVL as $V(y)$ (the invariant $L$ is fixed within
a solve). The junior tranche's pro-rata claim, rounded down in favour of
senior, is the \emph{conservative BPT valuation}:
\begin{equation}
  P_A(y) \;=\; \Bigl\lfloor\, f \cdot V(y) \,\Bigr\rfloor.
  \label{eq:PA}
\end{equation}
Two structural properties of $P_A$ matter (proved in \cref{sec:existence}):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Non-decreasing in $y$.
  \item Lipschitz in $y$ with constant $f \cdot \sup_y \mathrm{yb}(y)$,
    where $\mathrm{yb}(y) = V'(y)$ is the fair senior-share reserve at
    marginal price $y$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why the loop closes}
\label{sec:setup:circularity}

To run $F$ in a sync, the kernel needs $\JTraw$. But $\JTraw = P_A(y)$,
and $y = \STeff / N$, and $\STeff$ is the output of $F$. Reading the
dependency around the diagram:
\[
  y \;\xrightarrow{P_A}\; \JTraw \;\xrightarrow{F}\; \STeff
    \;\xrightarrow{\;\div N\;}\; y.
\]
Equivalently, the value $\STeff$ that the sync must produce has to satisfy
\begin{equation}
  \STeff \;=\; F\!\left(P_A\!\left(\STeff / N\right)\right).
  \label{eq:circularity}
\end{equation}
The loop is intrinsic, not an ordering bug. It exists because the junior
tranche's collateral \emph{contains senior shares}, so pricing the junior
collateral requires the senior price. If junior's coverage capital were
pure quote, $P_A$ would not depend on $y$ and there would be no loop.

The economic content is that the junior tranche is partly covering its
own collateral. Junior coverage props the senior rate up, a higher
senior rate raises the value of the senior shares sitting inside the
junior's BPT, and that raises the junior's measured coverage capacity. The strength of this
self-reference is the loop gain $g$, defined in \cref{sec:notation}
and bounded in \cref{sec:existence}.

\subsection{The fixed-point equation and the error function}
\label{sec:setup:fixed-point}

Let $x$ denote the search variable for the solved $\STeff$. The
fixed-point equation \eqref{eq:circularity} is
\begin{equation}
  x \;=\; F\!\left(P_A\!\left(x / N\right)\right).
  \label{eq:fixed-point}
\end{equation}
Define the iteration map and the error function:
\begin{align*}
  T(x) &\;:=\; F\!\left(P_A\!\left(x / N\right)\right), \\
  E(x) &\;:=\; T(x) - x.
\end{align*}
A self-consistent senior effective NAV is a root of $E$:
\begin{equation}
  E(x^*) = 0 \;\iff\; x^* = T(x^*).
  \label{eq:root-fp}
\end{equation}
The on-chain solver finds $x^*$ by integer bisection on $E$.
\Cref{sec:existence} proves that $E$ is continuous and strictly decreasing
on the search bracket, with a sign change across it, so the root exists,
is unique, and bisection converges to it deterministically.
\Cref{sec:solver} specifies the on-chain solver (bracket, tolerance,
iteration count, failure modes). Once $x^*$ is found, the published senior
rate is
\[
  y^* \;=\; x^* \,/\, N,
\]
written to the rate cache and returned by \code{getRate()}.
